# A2FMNLP

With the rapid development of network science, link prediction has become a prominent research topic with a wide range of application in areas such as social network analysis, recommendation systems, biological network inference, and cybersecurity. At its core, link prediction aims to estimate the likelihood of the existence (or emergence) of a link between two nodes in a network based on observed structural patterns, node attributes, or temporal evolution. Existing link prediction methods face significant challenges in handling heterogeneity and data sparsity, which limits their generalization ability and accuracy in real-world applications. While in recent years, Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have advanced this task, they still struggle to leverage the full complexity of heterogeneous graphs. In particular, most GNN-based methods are unable to effectively integrate node attribute features with topological structure, resulting in suboptimal embeddings and reduced predictive power. To overcome these challenges, this study proposes Attention-Based Adaptive Fusion for Multilayer Network Link Prediction (A2FMNLP), a novel framework that integrates multi-level feature fusion and structure-aware learning to enhance link prediction in sparse and heterogeneous networks. The proposed method learns both intra- and inter-layer representations of nodes and aggregates them through a relation-aware attention mechanism to generate more rich embeddings. Experimental results on real-world multiplexed network datasets demonstrate the effectiveness of A2FMNLP, showing consistent improvements over state-of-the-art baselines in terms of both link prediction accuracy and robustness, particularly in scenarios involving weak ties and heterogeneous structures.
